I've been reading the chapter on the hard side of a network: the smaller group of users who create most of the value for everyone else. Which means acquiring them is hard, keeping them happy is harder, and holding on to them once they're on the platform might be the hardest part of all.

While reading it, two failed networks came to mind "Vine" and "Mixer". if you aren't Gen Z, Vine was basically TikTok before TikTok, and Mixer was Microsoft's attempt to take on Twitch.

Mixer went straight at the hard side. It signed Ninja, the biggest streamer at the time, to an exclusive deal and expected his audience to follow him over. And I think Kick is running the same play right now. Pay the hard side a lot of money, and grow on the back of them. Kick also leans much harder into IRL content, with little to no censorship, which is it's own kind of bet.

Vine is the odd one out here. Mixer and Kick both had to go out and buy their hard side. Vine already had one. The viners were there, making the content, for free and it still lost them. Same problem from the opposite direction, which is probably why the chapter made me think of both at once. 

So these are the questions I'm sitting with:

- How did Mixer fail even after it got the hard side?
- Is Kick's version of the same strategy actually working?
- And why did Vine die, when short-form video went on to become the dominant format everywhere else?
