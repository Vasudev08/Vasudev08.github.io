I'm currently reading The Cold Start Problem by Andrew Chen, and in the opening chapters he draws on how social animals use group living to aid survival. Larger networks increase the probability of survival and health for the members within them but only up to a point. There's a tipping point where positive network effects kick in, and a point of saturation beyond which negative network effects take over, eventually reducing the population for a variety of reasons.

Reading that passage,  I couldn't help but think of NVIDIA's CUDA as its own version of a network effect. As more developers built on CUDA using NVIDIA GPUs, its popularity compounded it become both an industry and a research standard, and has since evolved into something closer to a moat. What I don't know yet it where CUDA's saturation point might be, or what a "negative network effect" would even look like for a developer ecosystem like this.

It would be interesting to watch how startups and established companies trying to challenge NVIDIA's dominance attempt to leverage network effects in this space. As the books argues, building a single, independent atomic network early on is critical and it's often what determines how much market share a challenger can eventually capture.
